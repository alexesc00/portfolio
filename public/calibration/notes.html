Calibration loop notes

How I read the hard problems

ProblemWhat the design does
Nothing is reusedKeeps each run's data with the version it tested, and each edit with its reason and expected change
AttributionShows answers, timing and feedback beside each tester's profile and the question's intent, and records the call
Small numbersShows counts like "4 of 10" instead of percentages
False comparisonsRecords who each batch of testers was and what changed between runs
Testers ≠ candidatesFeeds candidates' times, time-outs and blanks back so tester times can be adjusted
  • Besides the question, the platform and the testers, a low score can come from the rubric, the question's place in the test, dense wording, an inaccessible plot or a slow connection to a streamed tool.
  • When otherwise strong testers fail one question, the question is the problem.
  • A stronger batch of testers can make a bad edit look like a fix. If an unedited question also jumped, it was the testers.
  • A higher score isn't always a better question. Q2's jump from 57% to 90% could mean the edit gave the answer away, so the reviewer checks that the question's target testers still beat the rest.

Why these four wireframes

I drew only the moments where the brief's hard problems land.

WireframeWhy I drew it
WriteWriting happens in a doc today, and a question's record starts only once writing moves here
Decide recordThe team decides what to change here, and checks whether the last change worked
PromotionIt's the only screen about the whole assessment, not one question
Question historyProduction results come back to each question here
  • The testers' view isn't drawn, since it's the existing test plus feedback questions.

What I deliberately left out

  • I designed only what production feeds back into calibration, not the candidate's experience or client reporting.
  • Whether a high score predicts a good hire needs client feedback months after hiring.
  • A second scorer would show whether the rubric works without its author, but that needs another expert inside two weeks.
  • A model that predicts candidate time from tester data needs many more assessments, and a simple ratio works until then.

What I'd want to learn from the team

  • Why do people leave the platform for the doc and Slack? I assumed the doc is better for writing and discussion.
  • Who writes questions today, Colare staff or contracted engineers? I designed for an outside author and gave Colare the final say.
  • Who promotes today?
  • Does the client confirm which requirements we test before questions get written? I assumed not, so the author marks them in step 0.
  • Can engineering ship platform fixes between tester runs? I assumed so, inside the two weeks.
  • Can we record what happens inside the simulation tools? If so, the reviewer could watch every tester session.
  • Do calibrated questions get reused for other clients?
  • Is a client's hiring round a fixed window, or rolling? I assumed a set round, which is why fixes wait for the next version.
  • Who grades in production? I assumed someone other than the author, so the rubric has to work on its own.
  • How many candidates sit a typical assessment? I assumed enough to measure how they differ from testers.
  • Are candidates allowed to use AI, and do testers follow the same rule?
