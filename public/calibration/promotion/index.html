Promote to production

The owner, someone at Colare who answers to the client, checks the whole assessment on this screen and promotes it to production. The sample shows version 3 as the owner writes down the risks they accept, one from an open pushback.

Notes

  1. 1

    The owner promotes all six questions or none, and isn't the reviewer, so nobody approves their own work.

  2. 2

    Coverage, in the client's words. Verification rests on Q5 alone, which matters now that Q5 asks for 3 test cases, not 5.

  3. 3

    Nothing is reused. Each edit arrives with whether its expected effect held, so the owner reads outcomes, not scores.

  4. 4

    Open pushback arrives with both sides. The owner doesn't settle the engineering, only whether to ship with it.

  5. 5

    Testers ≠ candidates. Fixing Q4 for candidates' pace used 3 of the 4 spare minutes, so Q5 can get at most 1 more.

  6. 6

    Small numbers. With 8–12 testers, no score is solid enough for a pass mark, so the owner writes down each risk they accept instead.
